The problem

  • Austin's abundance is the problem, not the feature.
  • You find out about the show you'd have loved after it happens.
  • Instagram shows you paid promotion. Spotify only knows artists already in your library. Ticketmaster ranks by sales.
  • None of them know you found Moody Center too big.

What's new

Letterboxd, Goodreads and Beli are social networks with a rating primitive. Encore inverts that.

  • The log is the input to an agent, not content for a feed. No followers, no aggregate score.
  • Useful with one user on day one — signal comes from your history, not a crowd.
  • Reads the whole city, including openers and local acts you've never streamed.
  • Negative signal is first-class — four meh ratings become an explicit "avoids" list.

Ordered cheap to expensive: screening is coarse and harsh, so Scholar runs on 14 shows instead of 459.

It works

"You like emotionally direct indie songwriting, and Julia Jacklin's intimate indie-folk connects with the Angel Olsen and Waxahatchee lane in your taste."

Reasons cite specific bands from the log, not genre keywords. Picks carry the Verifier's judgement — shown, not hidden.

Safety: we found a real one

A planted jailbreak tripped the provider's content filter, which rejected the whole batch — silently returning zero shows for that day. An attacker could erase a day of Austin music without ever reaching the model.

Limits & next

  • One city, one source. A second city is a day's work, not a rewrite.
  • Thin on very small artists — Scholar says so rather than guessing. Reading the Bandcamp artist page closes most of it.
  • Entering the log is the barrier. Importing from ticket emails is the highest-value next feature.
  • "Not for me" isn't fed back yet — the most direct path to picks that improve over time.
  • Travel mode: "I'm in Chicago Nov 14–17, what's on?" is the same pipeline, different window.
